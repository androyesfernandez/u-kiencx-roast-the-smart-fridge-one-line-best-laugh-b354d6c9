## Winner line (verbatim, 155 chars)

> My smart speaker heard me say I'm broke and helpfully offered a payment plan for the speaker I already own, which is like being mugged by my own furniture.

## Critic's objections against the winner — and why the line was kept as-is

1. **Always-listening is a stock-adjacent trope.** True of the setup family,
   but B2's twist (payment plan *for the device itself*) is what the duel
   scored, not the setup alone — it beat every rival on freshness in the
   head-to-heads, including the fresher-angled A3.
2. **Premise is invented, not observed.** A payment plan for the speaker
   itself is a contrivance, but the brief rewards a turn/sting over literal
   reportage; the judge's criterion 5 asks for a real behaviour *of the
   thing*, and upselling is a real speaker behaviour — the absurd target is
   the joke, not a factual claim.
3. **The sting lands on furniture, not the gadget.** "Mugged by my own
   furniture" names the speaker's own category (it *is* the furniture in
   the room); the punchline stays on the gadget by synecdoche, and the duel
   found it the strongest, most quotable sting in the field.
4. **"Which is like" hedges and wastes characters.** Cutting it would save
   three words but break the line's rhythm and the surprise of the turn;
   the duel's margin-3 wins show the current phrasing already lands hard.
5. **Single-turn joke, no backup laugh.** A3's two-beat structure was
   weighed and still lost the head-to-head (margin 1 to B2); one decisive
   sting beats two softer beats under "laugh first".
6. **Cognitive load on the self-referential twist.** The half-second parse
   *is* the laugh — the realisation that the payment plan is for the
   speaker itself. A judge that skims past it still gets "mugged by my
   own furniture" as a standalone punchline.
7. **Finance joke, not gadget joke.** The humour engine is predatory
   upselling, but it only works because the *speaker* is doing it to its
   owner; a pushy salesperson cannot overhear you at home and sell you
   yourself. The gadget is not interchangeable.
8. **Field collision risk (speaker/upsell ground).** The specific angle —
   instalments for the device you already own — is distinctive within that
   ground, and the live Feed exposes every entry equally, so hiding the
   angle is not an option anyway.
9. **Five unused characters.** The line is complete at 155; padding it to
   160 would add throat-clearing the brief explicitly penalises
   (criterion 4). No edit improved the sting without changing the line.
10. **"Mugged" is a crime metaphor.** Mild, clearly figurative, and the
    brief bars only shock for its own sake. No other candidate carries a
    stronger sting without a comparable downside.
11. **Closest rival is a coin-flip away (B2 beat A3 by margin 1).** True,
    and the real judge's variance could flip that one game — but B2's edge
    rests on beating the other four decisively (margins 2–3), which is the
    best available evidence; swapping to A3 would trade a 0.74 mean for
    0.66 on the same evidence.
